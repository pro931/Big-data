01 — The data

Three months of invoices, cleaned down to the customers we can track

The dataset holds every product line sold by a UK-based online gift retailer between 1 December 2010 and 7 March 2011.

A third of the rows had no customer ID, so there was no way to tie them to a person. After dropping rows with missing values, 74,868 transactions from 1,853 customers remained. For each line I added the total price (quantity × unit price) and how many days had passed since the latest invoice in the data.

02 — The method

Every customer gets three scores, from 1 (best) to 4

Recency is the number of days since their last purchase. Frequency is how many separate orders they placed. Monetary is how much they spent in total. Each one is turned into a score of 1 to 4 using fixed cut-offs, and the three digits together form the customer's RFM score, so 111 is a customer who is best on all three.

The distributions already tell a story: more than half the customers (967) placed only one order.

05 — What to do with it

Each group needs a different conversation

The scores turn into clear marketing actions:

Best customers
Score 111 · 66 customers

Recent, frequent and high-spending. Protect them with loyalty rewards and early access rather than discounts.

Loyal, strong spenders
Recency 1–2, Frequency 1–2

Regular buyers just below the top. Personalised offers can move them into the best group.

Recent one-time buyers
Recency 1, Frequency 3–4

They have just tried the store. A timely follow-up is the best chance to earn a second order.

Fading customers
Recency 3, Frequency 4 · 439 customers

One purchase, a while ago, and low spend. Use low-cost win-back campaigns and accept that some are gone.

Appendix — How it was built

The PySpark pipeline

  1. Loaded OnlineRetail.csv into a Spark DataFrame and counted non-null values in every column.
  2. Dropped rows with missing values (112,803 → 74,868), parsed InvoiceDate into a timestamp, and added TotalPrice = Quantity × UnitPrice.
  3. Calculated each line's age in days from the latest invoice date.
  4. Grouped by customer: Recency = minimum days, Frequency = distinct invoices, Monetary = sum of TotalPrice.
  5. Scored each dimension from 1 to 4 with Spark UDFs and joined them into an RFMScore with concat.
  6. Summarised each segment's average recency, frequency and spend to find the most valuable groups.
